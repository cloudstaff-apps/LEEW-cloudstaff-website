1. Security

AI needs to be secure. Start there

The risks are real and they are already inside most businesses. Staff paste client data, financial records, source code, and internal documents into public AI tools without realizing where it goes. AI-built apps and automations get published before anyone has checked them. One misconfiguration can expose sensitive data.

Cloudstaff Q closes both doors. Your people get powerful AI inside a private environment, and everything they build is held inside rings of protection until it has earned its way out.

Four rings of protection

Everything starts in the innermost ring and moves outward one ring at a time, only when its security, access controls, data handling, and operational readiness have been reviewed. Nothing reaches the outside world until it is ready.

  1. 1 StagingPrivate development. Cloudstaffers only.A private development environment for prototyping, testing, and early-stage development.
  2. 2 Private productionInternal users only.A secure production environment accessible only to your Cloudstaffers and authorized onshore staff.
  3. 3 Limited productionCloudstaffers, authorized staff, and select customers.An extended production environment that selected customers or business partners can also reach securely.
  4. 4 Public productionOpen to all users.A fully public environment that staff, clients, and the general public can access.

AI Policy Builder

Before introducing AI into your business, your team needs clear guidance on which AI activities are permitted, restricted, or prohibited. The AI Policy Builder guides you through the key decisions and produces a practical governance framework tailored to your organization.

  • Customer and confidential data handling
  • Public AI tools versus private AI systems
  • Approved and restricted AI activities
  • AI usage for coding, content, reporting, and customer interactions
  • Compliance and security requirements

It defines clear rules for everyday scenarios such as summarizing meeting notes, drafting customer emails, analyzing spreadsheets, generating code, or uploading documents into AI tools. It can also distinguish between types of data and types of AI system, which is more workable than a simple allow or block approach. Finished policies go directly to your Cloudstaffers, who are notified automatically when anything changes.

Badge Pack AI

Even the best AI policy is ineffective if staff do not understand or remember it. Badge Pack reinforces your policy through a simple visual system of approved activity badges and compliance indicators, visible at the point of work.

  • Which AI activities are permitted
  • Which tools are approved
  • Which activities require approval
  • Which actions are prohibited

Badges can represent certifications, permissions, and approved AI tools, so AI access can be aligned to role, department, client requirements, and risk profile. They appear within Cloudstaff applications and timesheeting systems, an ongoing reminder that AI usage is governed, monitored, and aligned with your policy settings.

AI Access Controls

Not every AI platform is suitable for your business. Cloudstaff can apply AI website access rules at the network level, based on your company's AI policy.

  • Permit approved AI platforms
  • Block unauthorized AI tools
  • Restrict access to public AI systems
  • Reduce the risk of data leakage

Rules can be tailored by role, department, or client requirement. An accounting team might be allowed an approved spreadsheet plugin while developers use approved coding tools, and a support team can use private summarization tools but be blocked from entering customer records into public AI websites. Anyone who tries to open a blocked platform is told that access is restricted under company policy.
